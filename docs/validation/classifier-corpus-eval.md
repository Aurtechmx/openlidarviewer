# Derived classifier: corpus evaluation and ground-recall diagnosis

Method: `olv.class.derived-heuristic@3`, preset `derived-heuristic-v3`.
Corpus: `src/validation/classifierCorpus.ts` v1, digest
`sha256:2a0c0165…73b1db`.
Record: `validation/classifier-corpus/results-classifier-corpus.json`.

v3 adds a structural-verticality wall rescue. A tall point the per-cell
roughness rule sends to vegetation is re-examined with eigen descriptors
(`classification/geometryDescriptors` + `verticalityCue`) over a 1.0-unit
neighbourhood. A planar surface with verticality at or above 0.85 and 12 or more
neighbours is a wall face, so it is reclassified as building. It moved one scene
and no other. `walls-roofs` macro-F1 rose from 0.325 to 0.398 (building F1 0.423
to 0.638); the verticality and neighbour-count gates keep the rescue from firing
on natural steep or rolling terrain, which stay byte-identical to v2. The tables
below are the v3 record. Every non-wall scene reads the same as v2.

## What this evidence is

Every scene and every label here was generated by this project. The result is
one implementation measured against a generator the same project wrote. It
shows where the classification rules break and by how much. It is not field
validation, it is not survey truth, and it says nothing about accuracy on a real
scan. Returns are uniform-random in plan. There is no sensor geometry, no scan
pattern, no range noise, and the objects are boxes and clumps of points.

The corpus is frozen. Generation is seeded, coordinates are quantised to
millimetres, and one digest covers all twelve scenes. That digest is pinned in
`tests/classifierCorpus.test.ts`, which rebuilds the corpus and recomputes it,
and in `scripts/verify-classifier-corpus.mjs`, which compares it against the
record. A metric that rose because the scenes got easier would otherwise read
exactly like a classifier that improved.

### What the table already says

Four results stand out before any diagnosis.

1. `units-foot` scores identically to `units-metre`, class by class and count by
   count. The classifier carries no metre assumption of its own once the caller
   restates the length parameters in the file's unit.
2. Removing the colour cue (`urban-no-rgb`) and removing the return dimensions
   (`vegetation-no-returns`) leave their scenes unchanged to three decimals. On
   these scenes the geometry decides, and the cues change nothing.
3. `walls-roofs` scored 0.325 macro-F1 in v2 (0.398 in v3 with the wall rescue
   above) while its ground recall stays at 1.000. Wall returns fill a cell from
   the ground to the eaves, the per-cell roughness reads that as vegetation, and
   the vegetation bands then split one wall across
   three classes. Ground is untouched, so this is a vegetation-versus-structure
   failure and not a ground-filter failure. It is a separate question from the
   one below.
4. Vegetation precision is low across the corpus (0.319 to 0.653) while
   vegetation recall is high (0.914 to 0.978). The classifier over-assigns
   vegetation, mostly by putting misjudged ground into a vegetation band on the
   two natural-terrain scenes.

## Ground-recall diagnosis

Ground recall splits sharply: 1.000 on every planar scene, 0.636 on the 30 %
ridge, 0.402 on rolling terrain, and 0.000 on the low-outlier scene before the
change described below. Two different mechanisms produce that split.

Both are properties of the shared morphological core. The cross-implementation
record in `validation/cross-implementation/pdal-pipeline/` reports the same
shape for `classifyGroundSmrf` against `filters.smrf`: pooled ground recall
0.739, with plane at 1.000, ridge at 0.716, rolling at 0.448 and low blunders at
0.0099. That record compares two implementations. It does not say which one is
right about the ground, because nobody surveyed those scenes either. The
agreement between the two failure profiles is evidence that the mechanism sits
in the method both implement, not evidence that either matches the world.

### Mechanism 1: a below-ground return captures the grid minimum

Grayscale opening removes peaks, not pits. A return below the true surface is
therefore never carved out by the morphology that follows. It seeds the
minimum-elevation grid, the erosion carries it across the whole structuring
element, and the cut then pulls the surface down to it.

Evidence, all from the `low-outliers` scene at the published parameters:

| measurement | value |
| --- | --- |
| ground recall with the 30 blunders | 0.000 |
| ground recall with the same points, blunders removed | 1.000 |
| ground recall with 1 blunder | 0.985 |
| ground recall with 5 blunders | 0.706 |
| ground recall with 10 blunders | 0.615 |
| ground band needed to recover 99 % of true ground | 4.11 m |
| blunder depth as generated | 4.00 m |
| largest threshold on the published window ladder | 2.70 m |

The dose-response is the mechanism. One blunder damages its own cell and the
cells whose bilinear DTM patch touches it, costing 1.5 % of ground. Thirty
blunders across a 40 m scene put a pit inside every erosion window, so the
opened surface collapses everywhere and no ground survives. The critical density
is reached when blunders sit closer together than the largest window run, which
is 16 m at the published `maxObjectSizeM` of 20 m and a 1 m grid.

The ground band needed to recover the true ground, 4.11 m, matches the blunder
depth of 4.00 m. The surface is displaced by the blunder depth and by nothing
else. The cut fires at every ladder step because the largest threshold the
ladder reaches, 2.70 m, is below that depth: raising `elevThresholdM` to 5 m
recovers 0.94 of the ground without changing any other rule, which is the
criterion `drop > elevThresholdM + slope × run` behaving exactly as written.

The same mechanism explains a smaller number elsewhere. On the sibling
implementation the surface under the PDAL low-blunder fixture sits 3.67 m below
the generated bare earth on average, and every one of its 2,500 cells is more
than 0.5 m low.

### Mechanism 2: the threshold is linear in the run, the terrain is not

The cut fires when a cell stands more than `elevThresholdM + slope × run` above
the opened surface. That expression is a linear bound on how far terrain may
fall across a window. Real terrain with curvature does not fall linearly.

Measured on the corpus's own truth-labelled ground returns, gridded at the run
cell size:

| window radius (cells) | allowance | drop, `aerial-urban` | drop, `steep-terrain` | drop, `rolling-terrain` |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.45 m | 0.05 m | 0.58 m | 1.28 m |
| 2 | 0.60 m | 0.08 m | 0.87 m | 1.90 m |
| 4 | 0.90 m | 0.14 m | 1.50 m | 3.15 m |
| 8 | 1.50 m | 0.26 m | 2.75 m | 5.27 m |
| 16 | 2.70 m | 0.50 m | 5.21 m | 6.21 m |

The planar scene never crosses its allowance at any scale, which is why it
scores 1.000. The ridge crosses from the first step, because its flanks fall at
30 % and the tolerance encodes 15 %. Rolling terrain crosses at every step, and
the shape of the crossing is the part no slope constant can fix: the drop is
already 2.8 times the allowance at one cell, and between the two largest windows
it grows by 0.94 m while the allowance grows by 1.20 m. A slope small enough to
carve out a building at 16 cells is far too small at one cell, and a slope large
enough at one cell would admit buildings as ground.

Three candidate causes were tested and two are rejected.

| experiment | rolling ground recall |
| --- | ---: |
| grid cell 0.5 m | 0.436 |
| grid cell 1 m (published) | 0.402 |
| grid cell 2 m | 0.274 |
| grid cell 4 m | 0.196 |
| `maxObjectSizeM` 2 m | 0.899 |
| `maxObjectSizeM` 4 m | 0.755 |
| `maxObjectSizeM` 8 m | 0.482 |
| `maxObjectSizeM` 20 m (published) | 0.402 |
| `slope` 0.30 | 0.721 |
| `slope` 0.50 | 0.896 |

Grid cell size is not the cause. Quartering it moves recall from 0.402 to 0.436
and never approaches the planar result, so the feature wavelength against the
cell is not what fails. The window run is the cause: shortening the ladder to a
2 m maximum object recovers 0.899, and raising the slope tolerance to the
terrain's own 30 % recovers 0.721. On the ridge, a `slope` of 0.30 recovers
1.000 exactly, which is the flank gradient.

Both recoveries are trades and neither is a defect fix. A 2 m maximum window
stops carving out anything larger than 2 m, so buildings survive into the
bare-earth surface. A slope tolerance of 0.30 loosens the threshold everywhere,
including on flat ground where it would admit low walls, vehicles and
embankments as ground. This corpus does not measure that cost, so no default
moved. Tuning either number against these twelve scenes would fit the corpus,
not the terrain.

## What changed, and what it moved

Version 2 rejects low outliers before the grid minimum, and changes nothing
else. A cell whose lowest return sits more than `elevThresholdM + slope ×
cellSize` below the median of its measured neighbours falls back to its
second-lowest return.

The comparison is against the neighbours and not within the cell. A cell on a
building edge legitimately holds one ground return under several roof returns,
and a within-cell gap rule discards that ground and lifts the surface onto the
roof. A blunder is low relative to the terrain around it; a ground return under
a roof is not. A cell holding a single return is left alone, because one return
is one measurement and there is no evidence in it that it is a blunder. The
sibling ground filter takes the same position for the same reason. An earlier
version of this change did empty such cells, and the corpus caught the cost: one
low-vegetation point on `units-metre` lost its ground support and became
Unclassified, so `units-metre` and `units-foot` both fell by 0.0023 macro-F1.
That is why the rule reads as it does.

by-product and not a fix for mechanism 2.

## What is still broken

- **Rolling and steep terrain.** Ground recall stays at 0.402 and 0.636. The
  cause is diagnosed above and no default was changed to move it.
- **The blunders themselves.** All 30 gross returns are still classified ground.
  Height above ground is clamped at zero, so a return below the surface lands in
  the ground band, and the classifier has no low-noise class to put it in. v2
  stops a blunder from dragging the surface; it does not label the blunder.
- **Walls.** `walls-roofs` was 0.325 macro-F1 in v2. Wall returns read as rough
  and split across the vegetation bands. v3's structural-verticality rescue
  reclassifies planar-vertical neighbourhoods as building walls, which lifts it
  to 0.398 (building F1 0.423 to 0.638).
- **One blunder per cell.** The rejection is a single pass and falls back to the
  second-lowest return, so a cell holding two blunders stays low.

## Recommended experiments

1. Emit ASPRS 7 for a return that sits more than the rejection gap below the
   final bare-earth surface. This is the smallest change that would stop calling
   a multipath return ground, and the corpus already labels those points, so the
   effect is measurable the moment it lands.
2. Measure a slope tolerance that varies with the window radius, rather than one
   constant, against a corpus that also contains low walls, vehicles and
   embankments on flat ground. Mechanism 2 shows one constant cannot serve both
   scales, but the cost of a varying one is exactly the class this corpus does
   not carry, so the experiment needs those scenes before it can be judged.
3. Separate walls from vegetation with a verticality cue rather than per-cell
   roughness, and measure it on `walls-roofs`, which is the scene that isolates
   the confusion.

None of these should be attempted by moving a threshold against the twelve
scenes here. The corpus is small, synthetic and frozen, and a parameter fitted
to it would carry no evidence beyond it.
